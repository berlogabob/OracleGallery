Read this document alongside the Oracle Design System (oracle_design_system.html), which defines all colours, fonts, component states, and motion rules. The design system is the visual authority. This document is the structural authority. Together they are everything you need.

01

Who this site is for

The website serves two different visitors. Both matter. The site must work for each of them without either feeling like an afterthought.

Primary visitor
Someone who just left the installation
They scanned the QR code on their receipt. They are holding a printed slip that names their mark. They had an experience 5 minutes ago and want to understand it more deeply — and find themselves in the collective cloth.
They need: their mark explained, their session found in the cloth, a reason to linger and share.
Secondary visitor
Someone arriving cold
A friend sent them the link. They saw it on social media. They are a jury member, a professor, or a curious stranger. They have no prior context and need the project explained to them clearly and impressively.
They need: an immediate sense of what this is, the full story, and access to the same depth as the receipt visitor.

The site opens in the Document Register (cream background). The Installation Register (void, gold) appears only in hero sections and the cloth viewer. See the design system register guide for details.

Copy — AI transparency note
The mark the oracle selects is not a guess. It is the output of a small neural network trained on thousands of recorded voices — a system that has learned to map the acoustic properties of speech to emotional dimensions. It reads pitch, energy, rhythm, and the texture of breath between words. It cannot be misled by careful phrasing. Two people who speak the same words will receive different marks if they sound different when they say them.
Page 04 About /about Third priority

Context for the project: who made it, what course it is part of, and a plain-language explanation of the technical pipeline for the curious or technically minded visitor.

Jury members, professors, and press will read this page. Keep it honest and direct.

The project One paragraph: what Oracle is, what course it belongs to, when and where it was shown. Factual, not promotional.
The team Three names, three roles. No photographs needed. Simple list.
How it works — plain language A short technical overview written for a non-engineer. Covers: voice detection, speech-to-text, the LLM conversation, the emotion model, the plotter, the receipt. Each step in one sentence. See copy block below.
The AI systems Names the models and datasets used: Vosk (speech-to-text), Ollama (LLM), RAVDESS dataset, ONNX emotion model, KittenTTS. A sentence on each. This is the academic transparency layer.
Copy — How it works (plain language)
When a visitor speaks, the oracle uses voice activity detection to know when they have finished. Their speech is transcribed locally — nothing is sent to an external server. A language model generates the oracle's response and determines the emotional qualities of what it heard. A separately trained neural network maps those qualities to one of eight marks. A CNC plotter arm draws that mark onto a shared piece of fabric. A thermal printer produces the visitor's receipt. The session ends. The oracle resets.
03

The cloth viewer — technical note

This is the most technically complex feature on the site. It is also the most important one. It is what makes the website worth visiting after the installation rather than just a project description page.

The core interaction
A visitor types their session ID. Their mark appears, highlighted, in the collective cloth.
The cloth is a canvas or SVG element showing all marks drawn during the installation period, arranged in sequence. Each mark is an SVG drawn at a position on the canvas corresponding to where it was physically plotted on the fabric. When a session ID is entered, that mark highlights in gold. All others fade slightly. A label appears: the mark name, the session timestamp.
Data source: Firebase (same database used by the plotter pipeline)
Each session record needs: session_id · mark_name · svg_path · timestamp · x_position · y_position
The cloth viewer reads all records and renders them on load, then listens for new records in real time.
Session lookup: queries Firebase by session_id, returns the matching record, highlights it on canvas.

The arrangement of marks on screen should reflect the physical fabric — left to right, top to bottom, in the order they were drawn. The canvas dimensions should approximate the fabric's aspect ratio.

If the cloth viewer is not ready by the site launch date, the page can launch with a static image of the fabric and a "coming soon" placeholder for the session lookup. The rest of the site does not depend on this feature.

Social sharing

After a visitor finds their session in the cloth, a share prompt appears. The goal is to bring cold visitors to the site through people who had the experience — the receipt visitor becomes an ambassador.

ElementDetail
Share card image Auto-generated image: void background, the mark SVG centred in gold, mark name in Cinzel below it, "The Oracle That Wears Us · oracle.iade.pt" at the bottom. Sized for Instagram Stories (9:16) and square post (1:1).
Share text "The oracle drew this for me. The Oracle That Wears Us — [url]" — pre-filled, editable.
Share targets Instagram (image download + copy link), general copy link. No Twitter/X required.
Placement Appears only after a session is successfully found. Not visible on cold visit to The Cloth page.
